# NBD trust model

An executable model of [local trust](../device-enrollment.md#local-trust) in Device Enrollment ([TODO-17](../TODOs.md#todo-17)), with adversarial tests. It is informative: where the model and the prose disagree, one of them is wrong and should be fixed.

## What it models

- **Repos.** Each account's present device records and approval records, as an enumeration at a verified commit would prove them. Deleting a device record revokes it.
- **Grants.** Records in the issuer's space repo naming issuer, recipient, and space. The model never decrypts, so a grant may carry garbage, as a third party could not tell.
- **Membership.** The host's current eligible accounts per space.
- **`SpecClient`,** the acceptance layer of the specified design, and the whole design before grant standing: trust on first use under the single-root rule, approvals afterwards with acceptance persisted alongside its evidence, revocation by observed absence, and a re-enrollment hold bound to its exact candidate for clients that knew the account.
- **`InviterClient`,** the specified design: `SpecClient`'s acceptance plus [grant standing](../device-enrollment.md#grant-standing) and [invites](../core.md#invites), which decides whether the client may grant a space's key to a binding. Standing comes from an authorization: a published invite or capture record of the client's account, for that space, that names the binding, where each record authorizes exactly the bindings in its maker's first-contact lineage (accepted on first use, captured, or adopted for a never-pinned account, and their approved descendants; never re-enrolled) when it was made; an approval by a binding with standing (inheriting its scope), a 72-hour first-contact hold credited since the client accepted the binding, re-enrollment, or the client's own account. Invites of accounts with no devices are pending and capture the first devices met within `PENDING_INVITE_TTL`. Invites are prepared durably and retried exactly as prepared when a write fails (`World.fail_writes`), take effect only once published, and are never published for an account that is already a member. Invite records carry vouches to the account's other installations that have never pinned the invited account.
- **`WitnessClient`,** a variant for the [relay-witnessed repos](../proposal-reliable-detection.md) proposal, in `nbd_witness.py`: `InviterClient` deciding only on commits its own application's witness reports as published and reconstructed. `RelayWorld` adds commits (with a counter standing for every other collection, since a root covers the whole repo), a reference relay that publishes one ordered log delivered identically to every subscriber, and a malicious PDS that can serve one client a signed commit the relay never saw, at a new or a witnessed revision; commit without relaying, or at a revision it chooses; publish a `#sync` root and serve it only to some witnesses or replace it before they fetch; and withhold or stall fetches per witness. Revisions are millisecond timestamps the PDS chooses from its own clock (an honest PDS keeps them increasing), an honest relay can drop a commit, as under a rate limit, and the PDS can publish an earlier commit again at its own revision (`republish`), which a relay that passes on non-increasing revisions carries. A `Witness` per application tails the relay, reconstructs the device and approval collections at each exact root (by inversion for `#commit`, by fetching the exact root for `#sync` and after a failed inversion) or records a gap, records a stream gap when the relay refuses to resume after an outage longer than `RELAY_REPLAY_WINDOW` (a gap in every account's history, even one it resynchronizes at the revision it held, whose interval runs `REV_ALLOWANCE` past the relay's time when it resumed while the cursor after it moves only to the resync revision; finding 16), and keeps `WITNESS_RETENTION` of history. A commit whose revision is not above the account's previous one is a gap and an integrity alarm; one more than `REV_ALLOWANCE` ahead of its event's relay time is a gap without an alarm and does not raise the account's latest revision. Neither is accepted into the history (each is a gap at its place in relay order covering no revision of its own, so a cursor never moves back or leaps ahead), and the account's next commit is reconstructed as one after a gap. Each relay event carries the relay `time` at which the honest relay sequenced it, the same for every subscriber and on replay, distinct from when it reaches a given witness (a witness's `lag`); `RelayWorld.STAMP` keeps two non-reference behaviours for the limitation tests, a time stamped when the relay sends the event to each subscriber (`"sent"`) and a PDS-chosen time passed on (`"pds"`). A witness's frontier for an account is the relay time of the newest event it has processed in order (the firehose's other traffic is modelled as filling every instant), held at a commit's time while that commit awaits a fetch, which holds only its own account: later events of that account queue behind it, other accounts carry on, and after `FETCH_TIMEOUT` it becomes a gap. Frontier age is the witness's clock minus the frontier; a frontier more than `CLOCK_SKEW` ahead of the witness's clock is a fault, reported as an age exceeding any limit and latched until the witness is current again with its clock within `CLOCK_SKEW` of the relay's. Positions in an account's history are its repo revisions: witness history entries and gaps cover revisions, and installations' cursors, in backups and pairing transfers too, are revisions, so a restore or pairing can resume on another application's witness, with a gap for any revisions after the cursor that witness lacks (older than its retention, or before its history starts). The client returns `INCOMPLETE` while the PDS serves a commit its witness has not reported above its latest revision, alarms on a different commit at a witnessed revision and on one whose revision falls strictly between two commits the witness reconstructed consecutively (the later a `#commit` that inverted to the earlier; finding 12) unless its data equals the earlier one's (finding 13), raises nothing for one inside a recorded gap or one the witness reports as a gap, processes every witnessed commit of each pinned account in order (recording gaps, never skipping to a snapshot), and follows its own account's whole history from a persisted cursor: own-account alarms and notices for every binding in any reconstructed commit, permanent per-gap warnings the user can acknowledge one at a time, and a liveness warning when its coverage age (the frontier age at its last advance plus local time elapsed since it sent the request that produced that answer, wall-clock across restarts, unknown counted as stale) exceeds 24 hours. Requests and responses are separate steps (`request`, `deliver`): the witness computes an answer, with its frontier age, when the first page's request reaches it, serves later pages (`PAGE_SIZE`) from that answer, and the response to each page can arrive any time later; coverage counts from the first page's request, an answer older than the coverage already held does not move it back, and a response to a request sent before a restart is dropped, since the monotonic clock restarted. `sync` is a request answered at once. Fresh enrollment replays the account's history and presents every binding that appeared, with a gap for history the witness lacks; restores and pairings resume from the transferred revision on any witness and warn for intervals that witness lacks. `goal_violations` states the proposal's goal against client state. `ClientView` is what the PDS serves one baseline client, so the same forking PDS can be run against `InviterClient`.

There is no cryptography. A record signed by a binding is valid exactly when its creator held that binding's key, and tests create only records the creating party could sign. The model assumes one shared, current view of every repo, though each client keeps its own clock (`clock_offset`); partial views and delayed observations are left to the prototype in [TODO-18](../TODOs.md#todo-18). The relay-witness variant is the exception: each client there sees what the PDS serves it and what its own witness reconstructed.

## Running

From the repository root:

```bash
python3 -I -m unittest discover -s model -v
```

```bash
python3 -I model/mutation_check.py
```

```bash
NBD_WALKS=5000 NBD_STEPS=80 python3 -I -m unittest discover -s model -p test_walks.py
```

```bash
NBD_WITNESS_WALKS=1500 NBD_WITNESS_STEPS=60 python3 -I -m unittest discover -s model -p test_witness_walks.py
```

`test_traces.py` replays shared-view traces recorded by the TypeScript [prototype](../prototype/README.md), including recreated records and the viewer's clock moving back, through `SpecClient` and requires the same accepted bindings and holds after every step, so the two implementations cross-check each other. It replays the prototype's inviters traces through `InviterClient` the same way, also requiring the same standing in each space.

`test_inviters.py` tests `InviterClient` against each case in the proposal's "What it protects" table and "Before changing the baseline" list, including cross-space vouches, invites of known or held accounts, pending invites and their expiry, failed writes and retries, imports, and the remaining exposure (`test_limitation_`). `test_walks.py` also runs the walks over `InviterClient`, adding invites into two spaces, an account that installs only later, failing writes, and standing queries. Its invariants check every published vouched record against the walk's own record of each client's first contacts, as well as vouches, admitted spaces, first-contact credit, and a restatement of standing's sources.

`test_witness.py` tests `WitnessClient` against each row of the proposal's "What it achieves" table, running the same forking PDS against the baseline `InviterClient` where it applies, the "What stays out of reach" items (`test_limitation_`), the cases Astra raised (a validly signed commit offered by another source, a replaced `#sync` root staying a gap after resync, a restore from an old backup, snapshots around a deletion and recreation, the 25-hour deadline example, and gap acknowledgment against liveness), the event-time frontier, clock skew and revision cursors (`Frontier`: relay time against delivery time, a frontier ahead of the witness's clock, a pending fetch holding only its account, restores and pairings onto another application's witness), stream gaps (`StreamGaps`: Astra's trace of an impostor followed by the original revision replayed while the witness is away, a lower revision instead of the same one, no unrelayed-commit alarm across a stream gap, and a commit inside the gap's interval after resync still read), the reference relay's timestamps (`RelayTime`: a replayed event keeps the time it was sequenced, so a backlog shows; a relay stamping events when sent, or passing on PDS times, makes it look current, as `test_limitation_`s), transit time (`Transit`: an answer computed before an attack and delivered ten hours later, the same in two pages with the second page requested at hour eight, a late answer behind a newer one, and a response to a request sent before a restart), the revision rules (`Revisions`: an unrelayed commit between consecutive reconstructions, inside a gap, or above the latest revision; a commit an honest relay drops; far-future and non-increasing revisions, including one below a restored cursor), and the findings below (`test_finding_`), each showing the goal failing under the earlier rule and, where there is one, holding under the rule now. `test_witness_walks.py` runs seeded walks, 300 by default, over a forking PDS, `#sync` replacement and selective serving, withheld and stalled fetches, witness and relay outages, unreachable witnesses, witness clocks slow or fast against the relay within `CLOCK_SKEW` (and fast beyond it), stale-cursor restores onto either application's witness, wall-clock jumps, honest writes (some dropped by the relay, some at a revision up to 30 hours ahead), unrelayed or dropped commits served to one client with or without a later commit concealing them, impostors at non-increasing revisions, earlier commits republished at their own revision (including the one a witness last reconstructed), and requests whose paged answers arrive later, page by page, checking `goal_violations` (with `CLOCK_SKEW` of slack, finding 10), acceptance only on witnessed relayed commits, and no false integrity alarm (an unrelayed-commit alarm only on a commit the relay never published, or published out of order, between two consecutively relayed ones and never on an honestly dropped one; a non-increasing alarm only on a revision not above an earlier one; no latest revision ahead of the relay's time) after every step; lag walks add the conditions behind findings 1 and 2, which the rules now handle, and must keep the goal; finding walks also add the clock conditions behind findings 3 and 4 and must find a violation. The mutation check breaks one `WitnessClient` or witness rule at a time too (`--witness` runs only those).

`test_nbd_trust.py` tests `SpecClient` against the [security goals](../README.md#goals). Tests prefixed `test_limitation_` assert documented non-goals, such as root theft at first contact and a new contact meeting an account just after a takeover. `test_walks.py` runs seeded random walks, 1,500 by default: repo writes of every kind, elapsed time, queries, and backups exchanged between two of Alice's installations with different clocks, checking invariants after every step and shrinking any failure to a minimal trace. Walks may write any record with any key, as an attacker holding every key could, so their invariants are structural: acceptance only through approval paths, imports, or an elapsed hold; first use matching the single-root rule; holds credited no faster than real time and ending only for a stated reason; revocation, takeover alarms, and re-enrollment once their conditions hold. The docstring lists them. The mutation check breaks one `SpecClient` rule at a time and reports whether any test notices; every mutation should be caught, and the walks alone catch all of them.

## What it found

- **Single-root rule.** The prose required exactly one binding with no incoming approval. Approval records outlive deleted devices, so an honest reset that kept old approvals had two roots and could never complete. The rule now ignores roots that reach only deleted devices.
- **Durable approvals.** An earlier client recomputed acceptance from the approvals currently published, so deleting an approval record removed an accepted device. Acceptance is now persisted with its evidence, as the specification requires.
- **Holds bound to candidates.** An earlier client keyed holds by account, so a replacement candidate inherited an almost-expired hold. Holds now bind to the exact candidate, a replaced candidate's members are revoked, and imports merge holds only for identical candidates.
- **Anchors and witnesses.** Protecting newcomers with anchors drew repeated findings; the last showed one malicious PDS can manufacture its own witness. NBD accepted first-contact exposure instead; see below.
- **Takeover alarm.** The client revoked its own binding when its record disappeared but raised no alarm, despite the [required alarm](../device-enrollment.md#re-enrollment). It now alarms, including when the revocation arrives by import rather than direct observation.
- **Leftover approvals.** An attacker with create access could publish approvals from two of its own devices to an honest one; after the user deleted those devices, both deleted roots still reached the honest device, and new contacts declined it forever. Bindings without a present record now carry no authority of their own. A later review showed that leftover cycles (`M1 → M2 → M1 → Bob`) still left no root, so only present records can be roots now, and without one every record-less binding is a starting point.
- **Deleted intermediates.** Approval validation required each target's record to be present, so a contact offline while an account linked `B0 → B1 → B2` and removed `B1` never accepted `B2`. A target proven absent, or one the client had already revoked, is now accepted as an approval signer while staying revoked for grants; records must be published with their first approval so absence means deletion.
- **Stale holds.** Importing a backup taken during a hold brought the hold back after enrollment completed, and deleting the candidate left its hold pending. Holds now end on completion or cancellation, and no import brings them back while their reason for ending still applies. The fix first ended holds before checking imported bindings against the repo, so an older backup's deleted device restarted an active hold; imports now sync first.
- **Removed keys ended holds.** Random walks found that once every device of an account was revoked, an approval signed afterwards with a removed device's key, directly or through a key with no record, accepted the new device and ended the hold, so an attacker holding an old device's key skipped it. While every accepted binding of an account is revoked, approvals now accept nothing for it. A contact that missed an honest replacement before the old device's removal holds the new device too; linking flows therefore never prompt removing the replaced device.
- **Hold clocks.** Imports kept the earliest hold start time, comparing timestamps across installations whose clocks differ, so a clock 72 hours ahead finished a hold at once. Holds now carry credited elapsed time and never compare clocks.

## What the inviters variant found

- **Linked devices need their own first-contact hold.** The proposal started the first-contact hold only for bindings accepted on first use, with approved devices inheriting their signer's standing. A newcomer that saw Bob link a new phone during the old phone's hold, then remove the old phone, cancelled the only hold in the chain, and never gained standing for the new phone. Every binding accepted by first use, approval, or an invite record now starts its own hold, and still inherits its signer's standing if that comes sooner.
- **Removal freezes credit, it does not erase it.** A removed device stops earning first-contact credit but keeps what it earned, so a device it linked while it had standing keeps that standing. The client credits elapsed time before applying an observation, so credit earned up to the observation of a removal is not lost to lazy crediting. A removed device's key can still approve beside a live device (the documented gap), but what it approves inherits only the removed device's frozen credit, never a sibling's.

- **A later vouch must not extend an older invite.** Astra's third review found that the second revision granted standing from two independent facts, that a binding was vouched and that the account had once been admitted to the space, so a vouch made through one invite reached back into older invites: another installation's unvouched invite, or a pending invite that had expired. The tests and walks had checked the same two facts. Records now carry an `authorizes` list fixed when they are made, standing reads only that, and the walks check every authorization against a published record for its space; restoring the old rule is a mutation they catch.
- **Invite lifecycle.** Astra's fourth review found that a pending invite dropped unsent (its account was admitted some other way) kept its pending state, so the account's first device was later captured and granted under an invite that was never published. Dropping an invite now abandons it, cancelling its pending state and any capture queued for it, and new records queue behind unsent ones so a capture never publishes before its invite. The same review found that a later invite authorized only directly vouched devices, so a replacement phone linked from a vouched one waited out a hold in the next space; records now also authorize devices linked from vouched ones.
- **Transfers between installations.** Astra's fifth review found, in the prototype, that importing a stale backup restored an abandoned pending invite, and that adoption and imports still required a direct vouch for a replacement device. The model already behaved correctly in both cases, since it never transferred pending state and applied the descendant rule to adoption. It now records abandonment persistently (and transfers that record), refuses captures for abandoned invites or invites not yet published, and judges imported authorizations only after merging the approval evidence they rest on, an ordering the earlier code got wrong without a test noticing. Two of these checks are defense in depth: they matter only if stale state returns by some other path, so their tests inject it directly.
- **New spaces with people met another way.** After adoption, a new space waited whenever its creator had met someone some other way, such as a fellow member of another space, in the last three days, because only devices first met through an invite could be authorized. Invites now authorize any device in the inviter's first-contact lineage (accepted on first use, however and whenever, or captured, or adopted for a never-pinned account, and their approved descendants), never a reset's new devices. The tests that held such invites now authorize them, the protection tests became the reset case, and a mutation restoring the narrow rule is caught. One effect is safer than before: an installation that adopts another's authorization of an account now knows the account, so a later takeover of it is a reset there, held even in a space it invites the account into.

## What the relay-witness variant found

The model supports the proposal's central claim within its assumptions: in walks and tests, every impostor an honest `WitnessClient` accepted was in, or left a gap in, the history each of the owner's installations read, and the owner alarmed or warned within 24 hours, under forking, `#sync` replacement, selective serving, withheld fetches, outages, stale restores, and clock jumps while running. It also found the following, each with a `test_finding_` (or `test_limitation_`) case.

1. **A witness can be live while far behind the relay.** The first draft's live test (an open subscription, a frame within 60 seconds, every delivered event processed) holds throughout a replay of a backlog after an outage shorter than the relay's window, or for a consumer that falls behind, because frames keep arriving. Its frontier age stays near zero while the impostor's commit is undelivered, so the owner neither alarms nor warns 24 hours after the inviter accepted it. The model first fixed this by also requiring the witness to have processed through a relay head observed within the last 60 seconds. The proposal now measures the frontier by the relay's own `time` on the events processed, which needs no head request; the model follows it (the `LIVE_RULE` switch is gone), the finding's test now holds the goal under the default rule, and frontiers from delivery time instead of relay time are a mutation the tests catch.
2. **Fresh enrollment sees only the history its witness has.** An impostor captured through a pending invite and deleted more than `WITNESS_RETENTION` before the account's first installation, or before the installation's witness began following the account (a new application, or a witness rebuilt from snapshots), is neither presented nor a gap: the replay starts clean. History the witness does not have back to the account's creation should be a gap warning at enrollment, as an interval older than retention is at restore. The proposal adopted this, and it is now the default (`FRESH_HISTORY_GAP = False` restores the earlier behaviour for the finding's tests).
3. **Monotonic clocks that stop in suspend.** Coverage age uses a monotonic clock while running. On platforms where that clock pauses during suspend, a phone asleep for a day with its witness unreachable on waking reports a coverage age of minutes. Elapsed time should include suspension, or a resume should count as a restart.
4. **The goal assumes honest clocks.** A clock set back by less than the time an installation was stopped shortens its coverage age without making the interval unknown; with the witness unreachable, the warning comes late. Hold timing states this assumption; the goal does not.
5. **A stalled fetch either stalls everyone or vanishes.** The proposal does not say how long a witness waits on a `#sync` fetch, or whether the event counts as processed meanwhile. If a pending fetch holds the stream, one PDS that stalls fetches keeps the witness from ever being live, and every user of the application sees a liveness warning they cannot dismiss. If it waits aside without a bound, the commit is neither reconstructed nor a gap while coverage stays fresh, which answers review question 4 with yes. A bounded wait after which the commit is a gap, without holding other accounts' progress, keeps the goal; the bound then adds to the deadline unless the account's own frontier does not pass the pending commit. The proposal now does exactly that, and the model's default (`Witness.PENDING = "account"`) follows: with the frontier held at the pending commit's relay time, even an unbounded wait warns within 24 hours, and the timeout only turns the wait into a gap. The `"stream"` and `"aside"` settings keep the two failing readings for the finding's tests.
6. **A removed key's impostor is a notice, not an alarm.** A removed device's key approving a device beside a live one makes the owner's installation accept it by approval, so the alarm the goal promises is only the baseline own-account notice, though the witnessed history shows the approval appearing after its signer's removal.
7. **Forks are caught only at a witnessed revision.** A PDS that serves a client a signed commit at a revision the relay never carried, then relays higher revisions, was never alarmed on, only ignored. The proposal now alarms on a served commit whose revision falls strictly between two consecutive reconstructions, which the model follows (with finding 12's narrowing); dropping that check, or alarming on any unreported revision below the latest, is a mutation the tests catch.
8. **Honest writes race `#sync` fetches.** A root covers the whole repo, and an honest PDS serves only its current repo, so any write in any collection between a `#sync` and the witness's fetch leaves a gap on an account nobody attacked. The relayed `#commit`s after the `#sync` invert back to its root, so a later snapshot plus their inverted operations could reconstruct it; the proposal's "a later snapshot does not reconstruct an earlier root" rules that out. (Not modelled as a fix.)
9. **The inviter's witness is trusted too.** The goal assumes the relay and *your* application's witness are honest, but the accepting client decides on its own application's witness. A dishonest or compromised witness of another application lets its clients accept a commit the relay never carried, and the owner has nothing to see. Every application's witness serving a member who meets you is in your trust base, and as an online server it is a different target from shipped application code.
10. **The clock-skew allowance stretches the deadline.** Under the event-time frontier, a witness clock slow against the relay by less than `CLOCK_SKEW` is no fault but understates frontier age by up to that much, so the liveness warning can come up to 5 minutes after the 24 hours the goal promises. The goal holds with `CLOCK_SKEW` of slack, which the walks allow; the proposal should either say "24 hours plus the skew allowance" or have the witness add `CLOCK_SKEW` to the frontier age it reports.
11. **A slow witness clock is a fault only while the witness keeps up.** The skew check compares the frontier with the witness's clock, so it sees a slow clock only while the frontier is current. Walks found that a fault which clears as soon as the frontier falls more than the allowance behind the slow clock lets a stalled witness understate its frontier age by the clock's whole error (30 minutes in the walk); the model now latches the fault until the witness is current again with its clock in agreement, one reading of "until it is resolved". Even so, a clock that goes slow while the witness is down or behind is never a fault, and its error adds to the deadline. The goal holds only for witness clocks within the allowance, an assumption the proposal states ("servers with synchronized clocks") but the check cannot enforce; the walks keep slow witness clocks within it.

12. **A `#sync` after a dropped commit is a false alarm.** The proposal says a commit an honest relay dropped never lands between consecutive reconstructions, because the next commit's operations fail inversion. A `#sync` has no operations: if an honest relay drops a commit and the PDS's next event is a `#sync` (after a migration, say), the witness reconstructs it by fetch right after the previous reconstruction, and a client holding the dropped commit alarms though the PDS and relay are honest. The walks found this. The model brackets an unrelayed commit only when the later reconstruction is a `#commit` whose operations inverted to the earlier one (`Witness.CONSECUTIVE = "inverted"`); `"any"` keeps the earlier wording for the finding's test, and the walks catch it as a mutation. The proposal adopted this: consecutive now means the later is a `#commit` whose operations inverted to the earlier.
13. **A dropped commit that changes no record still brackets.** A commit with the same data as the one before it (re-signed after a signing-key rotation, say) leaves the next commit's operations inverting to the witness's state when an honest relay drops it, so a client holding it alarmed. The proposal adopted the exemption the model suggested: a commit that changes nothing hides nothing, so a served commit whose data equals the earlier reconstruction's raises no alarm. The model follows it (`WitnessClient.EXEMPT_UNCHANGED`; `False` keeps the earlier text for the finding's test), a hidden commit that changes records still alarms, and dropping the exemption or comparing with the later commit instead are mutations the tests catch. The walks never write such a commit.
14. **"The relay published nothing there" is not quite what the witness knows.** It knows the relay published nothing *before the later reconstruction in relay order*. A commit relayed afterwards with a lower revision (itself a non-increasing alarm when the witness reaches it) is called unrelayed if a client is served it while the witness is behind. Either way the PDS misbehaved, so the alarm is right; the proposal now names the out-of-order case. Likewise a served commit the witness already reports as a gap (non-increasing or far ahead) is neither unrelayed nor `INCOMPLETE`: read literally, the earlier "above the witness's latest revision, `INCOMPLETE`" left every client meeting an account whose head is a far-future commit waiting until the PDS next wrote. The proposal adopted this, and the model treats a commit the witness reports as a gap as reported.
15. **The revision rules as modelled.** Revisions were a counter in the model, so the non-increasing rule was never exercised; it is now, including against a restored cursor (the alarm and gap are read by an installation whose cursor is already past the commit's revision) and after a stream gap (resynchronizing at a relay head below the revision held is the same alarm). A PDS whose clock stays more than `REV_ALLOWANCE` ahead makes every commit a gap without an alarm, so its account has no reconstructed history until the clock is right (an honest relay rejects such commits anyway). The commit after a far-future one is reconstructed by fetch, so finding 8's race applies to it: an honest write before the fetch makes it a gap too.

16. **A stream gap's interval must reach past the revision it resynchronizes at.** Astra found that a stream gap at the revision the witness already held was exempt ("it missed nothing"): while Alice's witness is away past the relay's window, her PDS publishes an impostor that Bob's client accepts, then the original commit again, which a relay that passes on non-increasing revisions carries. On resync the witness finds the revision and commit it held, records an empty interval, and Alice neither alarms nor warns. The proposal now says a stream gap is a gap in every account's history, including at the same revision, and the model follows. But the interval the proposal defines, "from just after the last revision the witness reconstructed for the account, through the first revision it reconstructed again", still misses the impostor whenever the relay carried a revision above the one the witness resynchronizes at: with the same revision it is empty, and if the PDS follows the impostor with any commit between the held revision and the impostor's (`test_lower_revision_after_an_impostor_during_a_stream_gap_is_a_gap`), it ends below the impostor. Any revision another witness could have accepted during the gap is at most `REV_ALLOWANCE` past the relay time at which the witness resumed, so the model's stream gap runs from just after the last revision it reconstructed through that bound (or the resync revision, if higher). Installations' cursors after a stream gap move only to the resync revision, not the end of the interval, or a commit relayed just after the resync with a revision inside the interval would be skipped; a stream gap also breaks consecutive reconstructions, so the impostor, relayed during the gap, is never called unrelayed. Suggested wording for the gap paragraph: "A stream gap is the exception: its interval runs from just after the last revision the witness reconstructed for the account through `REV_ALLOWANCE` past the relay time at which it resumed, or through the revision it resynchronizes at if that is higher, since a relay that passes on non-increasing revisions may have carried higher revisions while it was away. An installation's cursor moves past a stream gap only to the revision the witness resynchronized at." Restoring the exemption, ending the interval at the resync revision, moving the cursor to the interval's end, and letting a stream gap keep reconstructions consecutive are mutations the tests catch; the walks, which now republish earlier commits, do not reach the attack's ordering.
17. **Revisions are the same at every witness only outside stream gaps.** The walks found that a witness resynchronizing after a stream gap at a commit whose revision an earlier commit already had (one it never saw, followed by a non-increasing commit at that revision) reconstructs a different commit at that revision than a witness that followed the stream throughout, which recorded the later one as a non-increasing gap. The witness cannot tell, and nothing goes wrong: the stream gap covers that revision, a client served the other commit raises a fork alarm, and both commits were published. The proposal's "revisions ... are the same at every witness" needs "outside a witness's recorded gaps". (The walk invariant was relaxed to "a commit the relay published at that revision"; this is not new behaviour, the earlier walks just never hit it.)
18. **Transit and pages.** Coverage counting from the request's dispatch holds in the model, and an answer delivered ten hours late, or with its second page requested eight hours after the first, no longer refreshes coverage; aging from the response's arrival, or from the last page's request, are mutations the tests catch. The first page's request is the right origin because the model's witness computes one answer, with one frontier age, when the first page's request reaches it, and serves later pages from it; the proposal could say so ("an answer in several pages carries the frontier age computed for its first page"). Two details the proposal leaves open, which the model settles: an answer whose frontier is older than the coverage already held (a response overtaken by a newer one) does not move coverage back, since it does not advance the frontier; and a response to a request sent before a restart cannot be timed by the restarted monotonic clock, so the model drops it (counting its transit by the wall clock, as a restart does, would also work).
19. **The reference relay's timestamps.** The model's relay already stamped each event once, when it sequenced it, and kept that time for every subscriber and on replay, as the proposal now requires. A relay that stamps events when it sends them to each subscriber makes a 30-hour backlog report a frontier age of zero, and one that passes on PDS-chosen times lets the PDS stamp events with the time they will reach a witness it knows is behind; in both, Alice neither alarms nor warns 24 hours after Bob accepted an impostor (`test_limitation_relay_`). A PDS-chosen time also turns `REV_ALLOWANCE` into a check of the PDS against itself: with its clock ahead, its far-future revisions are accepted.

With revision cursors, switching witnesses is safe: a backup restored, or a pairing transferred, onto another application's witness resumes at the same place in the account's history and catches an impostor added and deleted after the cursor, and a cursor stored as an index into one witness's log is a mutation the tests and walks catch.

Two smaller points: coverage age must *exceed* 24 hours, so a device accepted at the frontier's exact instant warns an instant after the 24 hours the goal promises; and replaying history from a cursor must not revoke a binding at a commit older than its record (the baseline's revocation rule already says so), which a test now covers.
