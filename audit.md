# NBD: Grant Publication and Auditing

The signed [grant](core.md#grant-format) is the audit record. Publishing it in the issuer's own space repo lets an affected account discover substitution through another PDS: if Bob grants to keys forged by Alice's PDS, Alice can obtain Bob's grant and compare its exact recipient binding with the devices she has approved.

## Evidence

A manifest is `{ version: 1, space, blocks: CID[] }`, with `blocks` a set of exact protocol values. Each referring field determines its block's required type. Issuers MUST include:

- Both parties' exact bindings and the signed approvals on the paths used for issuance.
- Configuration and both membership decisions.
- For each party's account, the signed repo commit and proof path against which the issuer [proved](device-enrollment.md#repo-observations) that party's device record present. Readers verify these as historical commits; after signing-key rotation they may become unverifiable, which is an audit gap, not invalid evidence.

The grant header's binding and host-state references MUST be in its manifest; the package includes the manifest itself. A manifest MUST NOT reference its enclosing grant.

A space has one key, so each grant is an audit record for access to all of the space's content, including content posted before the recipient joined.

Stop at bindings the issuer accepted directly. Private trust history stays private. Configuration and membership blocks MUST belong to this space; bindings, approvals, and repo commits have no space. Readers use their own trust/history and may need additional evidence; the bundle does not promise universal verification from scratch.

Issuers MUST publish actual bytes as immutable [evidence records](core.md#records) in the issuer's space repo, readable without the granted key or substituted private key. Batching/shared same-space records are allowed; issuers MUST preserve exact bytes and all referenced blocks. Links back to a withholding source PDS are insufficient. The manifest reference binds this package through the HPKE header and grant signature. Absence of private provenance is not missing required public evidence.

## Publication

Each issuance has immutable grant/evidence bytes and record identity, a durable state (`prepared`, `outcome-unknown`, `acknowledged`, or `stopped`), and retained publication/uncertainty facts. Issuers MUST follow P1–P8. `Ready` means evidence publication is complete and [grant authorization](core.md#grant-authorization) has just returned `OK` using the exact evidence and trust decision recorded for this issuance. Every attempt releasing grant bytes requires `Ready`, including retries and direct forwarding.

| Step | State/event | Required action and next state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | New issuance | Durably save exact signed grant, evidence, identity, and authorization decision before upload; enter `prepared`. |
| P2 | `prepared`, evidence upload/retry | Upload actual evidence bytes first; resume partial uploads without changing bytes. Remain `prepared` until grant submission. |
| P3 | `prepared`, `Ready` | Persist `outcome-unknown` before uploading the grant at its exact identity. |
| P4 | `outcome-unknown`, result/reconciliation | An authenticated acknowledgment of the exact write or an authenticated read of identical bytes establishes `acknowledged`. Different bytes are an integrity error: enter `stopped`. Absence, timeout, or failed reads leave `outcome-unknown` and exposure uncertain. |
| P5 | `outcome-unknown`, retry | Require `Ready`, then resend only the same grant bytes/identity; remain `outcome-unknown` until P4. Duplicate publication is one grant. |
| P6 | `acknowledged`, direct forwarding | Require `Ready`, then forward the same published record; remain `acknowledged`. Without acknowledgment direct forwarding is forbidden. |
| P7 | Any active state, lost authority/missing required evidence/changed evidence or trust decision/cancellation | Durably enter `stopped`; release no further grant bytes for this issuance. A new issuance requires new preparation and authorization, not reinterpretation of the old record. |
| P8 | `stopped`, late publication result | Retain new acknowledgment or uncertainty evidence without resuming delivery or erasing earlier outcomes. |

An authenticated integrity error at a prepared record's existing identity also uses P7. On restart, issuers MUST restore these states and MUST NOT assume an interrupted write failed. A previously acknowledged issuance that later stops retains its acknowledgment and exposure facts. Publication acknowledgment proves neither durable availability nor recipient decryption; failure, cancellation, deletion, or absence MUST NOT be treated as proof that released keys were unexposed. Previously published records and uncertain outcomes MUST remain retained after stopping.

## Retention and access

Except for disposable [key requests](core.md#key-requests), publishers MUST retain published immutable records and exact supporting evidence indefinitely. Each account MUST keep the approvals connecting its unrevoked devices in its public repo; issuers MUST keep the grants and evidence they used. Publishers MUST preserve these through migration, replacement, conflict resolution, revocation, and membership changes, and MUST NOT prune referenced evidence.

Permissioned evidence follows normal Spaces access rules. Former members may keep/audit synced copies but have no guaranteed further access, even to grants addressed to them. Retention does not extend authorization or guarantee hosting, availability, completeness, or freshness.

## Archived grants

Historical reading MUST use [grant acceptance](core.md#grant-acceptance) in archive mode. Later membership removal or device revocation alone does not invalidate a historical grant. The recipient MUST still authenticate its exact bindings, signatures, required evidence, and epoch commitment, and accept the issuer binding under [local trust](device-enrollment.md#local-trust) before first key acceptance.

For A4 in either mode, the referenced membership decisions MUST authorize the exact issuer and recipient accounts, and the referenced configuration and approval evidence MUST permit the grant on its recorded basis. A known failure of those authorization checks returns `BLOCKED`; invalid or missing required evidence returns `INVALID` or `INCOMPLETE`. A newer eligible decision MUST NOT repair an ineligible decision recorded in the grant.

Assess historical authorization separately using retained history, without requiring either party to remain enrolled. Clients MAY read an archive with uncertain historical authorization once all mandatory checks pass; lack of proof about issuance time or the issuer's then-known state is not missing required public evidence. Timestamps, old approvals, and decryption do not establish that issuance preceded revocation or that the issuer knew globally current state.

Clients MUST report three separate assessments: cryptographic/binding/evidence validity (`valid`, `invalid`, `incomplete`), historical authorization (`supported`, `uncertain`, `contradicted`), and audit coverage/gaps. A retained local issuance decision with its then-known state can support that decision; no trusted timestamp service is required. Historical reading MUST NOT reset pins/revocations, restore current authority, or authorize new issuance, retries, or direct delivery.

## Direct sync

Auditors MUST discover repos through authenticated host writer discovery, available membership information, and retained participant/issuer history, including known former participants while access permits. Auditors MUST resolve each account's current repo host and sync directly with Spaces credentials bound to the requester's key with HTTP Message Signatures, as the [pinned Spaces revision](core.md#spaces-dependency) specifies. Auditors MUST use permissioned-repo integrity/commit rules, not public MST proofs; public device/revocation records use public-repo sync. See the [Spaces sync boundary](https://github.com/bluesky-social/proposals/tree/main/0016-permissioned-data#the-sync-boundary-writer-set).

Auditors MUST atomically persist each authenticated repo checkpoint, retained record IDs/CIDs, audit cursor, pending evidence requests, and gaps with processed evidence. Notifications schedule catch-up; periodic discovery/sweeps recover missed notifications. Discontinuity, migration, or invalid checkpoints require reconciliation without erasing history or marking skipped intervals complete. Deleted immutable audit records are gaps, not revocations; only a deleted device record [revokes](device-enrollment.md#device-revocations) anything; deleting a disposable key request is not an audit gap. Renew credentials normally; failed renewal proves neither an empty repo nor a clean audit. Auditors MUST stop retrieval when denied and preserve permitted local copies.

Clients MUST audit grants to the local account without waiting for content keys, promptly when they learn of them, using notifications and periodic sweeps of known issuers' repos. Together with [own-account history](#own-account-history), which catches the substituted device record itself, this audit is the account holder's defense against [first-contact](device-enrollment.md#local-trust) key substitution and against [re-enrollment](device-enrollment.md#re-enrollment) by a malicious PDS that hides the deletions from the account's own installations; the grant audit adds which spaces received the key. Clients MUST visibly alarm on a grant to a binding of the local account that this installation has not accepted, and on approvals or device records in the local account's public repo for bindings it has not accepted. It MUST likewise alarm on an [invite record](core.md#invites) in any space it can read that names, in `bindings` or `authorizes`, a binding of the local account it has not accepted: an inviter vouching for such a binding may already have granted to it. An installation accepts its own account's bindings as follows: its own binding, generated locally or restored with its keys from the user's own authenticated [backup or pairing transfer](device-enrollment.md#backup-and-transfer), needs no approval; neither do pins copied by such a transfer; every other own binding needs an approval path from one of these, as for any account. An account's first device therefore never alarms on grants to itself, while a substituted binding, which no own installation generated, transferred, or approved, still does. An installation that lost its state and re-pinned under [local trust](device-enrollment.md#local-trust) has reported that loss and may not recognize bindings its earlier state accepted; it treats such alarms as inconclusive rather than suppressing them. An installation [starting over](device-enrollment.md#total-device-loss) after total device loss has no history for its own account either. It presents the account's other bindings as the devices to remove rather than raising an alarm for each, since it cannot tell the user's lost devices from anyone else's; the user's recognition of them is the check. Once it has verified a binding revoked, grants to that binding are inconclusive history: it preserves them and shows them on request, without alarming. It MUST still alarm on any own binding it has not accepted that remains present after the removal or appears later, and on grants to such a binding. A PDS can hide records from its own account's reads, so the account's own public records are read through its [witnessed history](device-enrollment.md#witnessed-history), not from the PDS alone, and grants in other issuers' repos remain a signal the PDS cannot alter. Auditors MUST check issuer signatures/bindings, exact recipient bindings, and retained own approval history. Distinguish unauthorized bindings, revoked bindings, malformed evidence, and insufficient history. Auditors MUST preserve evidence and alert on known unauthorized bindings; missing remembered approval is inconclusive when history is missing. Observations without a signed grant do not establish issuance. Among signed commits of an account's repo, whether retained locally or found in grant evidence, two different commits at one revision, or commits showing a binding present, then absent, then present again, raise an integrity alarm. Because grant evidence carries the commit each issuer relied on, first-contact substitution or a forked history can also surface this way to another member who verified a different commit at the same revision; a PDS that forges only at revisions no other member verifies avoids it, so this is detection by chance, not a defense. Repo commits do not chain, so other divergence between clients' views is not detectable this way; [witnessed history](device-enrollment.md#witnessed-history) is what detects it ([limits](device-enrollment.md#revocation-authority)). A signature attributes a statement to a key, not PDS misconduct or recipient decryption.

An approval by a compromised own installation passes every signature and approval check, so the account holder is the only party who can recognize it. Each installation MUST therefore visibly notify the user of every new or removed binding of the local account that it did not itself approve, including bindings approved by another own installation, learned from any of: an approval, device record, or device-record deletion in its public repo, a grant to an own-account binding in another issuer's repo, or state imported from another own installation or a backup. Bindings already in the state an installation is paired or restored from are its starting point, not changes, and need no notice; bindings an import adds to an installation's existing state do. Notices identify the binding and the approving installation when known; one notice covers a binding across all spaces. Notices for additions MUST offer removal, which takes the user to their PDS to delete the device record, [revoking](device-enrollment.md#device-revocations) it, and to end its session ([ending access](core.md#ending-access)). Removal does not [rotate](core.md#no-rotation) space keys, and the notice SHOULD advise changing the password; applications SHOULD also offer to [replace](core.md#replacing-a-space) affected spaces, since an unrecognized device suggests compromise. These notices are not integrity alarms and add no approval gate. They protect only users with at least one honest installation online to receive them.

Applications MUST let users inspect observed grants to any member, including unexpected admissions. Show the exact recipient binding, issuer, membership decision used, including evidence gaps. Correlate grants with authenticated membership changes. Grants to an unexpected host-authorized member remain protocol-valid when all required checks pass; audit evidence does not establish users' consent to that admission.

Detection requires an honest issuer's evidence, eventual access, and enough local history. Withholding, collusion, isolation, or lost read access can prevent it; matching observed grants do not prove all grants were seen. Auditors MUST report gaps and continue background catch-up without blocking unrelated operation. [Grant checks](core.md#grant-authorization) still apply. Detection through grants has no guaranteed deadline and cannot undo disclosure; detection of an impostor device through [own-account history](#own-account-history) has a 24-hour bound, but cannot undo disclosure either.

Audit records expose device/account relationships and transferable device signatures to authorized readers. Publishers MUST NOT include private trust history, other-space history, credentials/tokens, DPoP keys, or plaintext.

## Own-account history

Each installation watches its own account's history, as its [witness](device-enrollment.md#witnessed-history) reconstructs it, and alarms on any device it did not accept, even one that was there only briefly. Because every client accepts devices only from that same relay-published history, an impostor any honest client accepts appears in the history the account's installations read, or leaves a gap there. If any honest client accepts a device as the user's that none of the user's installations generated, transferred, or approved, a running installation alarms within 24 hours, or warns that it cannot check, and one that was not running does so when it next runs. This holds against a malicious PDS, including the user's own, as long as the reference relay, the user's application's witness, and the witness of the client that accepted the device are honest, and clocks are honest ([hold timing](device-enrollment.md#hold-timing)). It is detection, not prevention: for the member who invites the impostor into a space, the alarm reports a disclosure that has already happened.

### Monitoring and history

An installation tracks two separate things for its own account:

- **Monitoring frontier.** The latest relay time up to which the installation has processed every commit the witness received for the account, each one either reconstructed and applied or recorded as a gap. A recorded gap does not hold the frontier back: once the installation has recorded and surfaced it, the frontier moves past it. The installation's **coverage age** is the frontier age the witness reported ([witness progress](device-enrollment.md#witness-progress)) when the frontier last advanced, plus the time elapsed locally since the installation sent the request that produced that answer, or, for an answer in several pages, the first page's request, so an answer delayed in transit cannot make old coverage look fresh. A paged answer carries one frontier age, computed when the first page's request arrives. A late answer whose frontier is older than coverage the installation already holds does not move coverage back, and a response to a request sent before the installation restarted is discarded. Elapsed time is counted from a monotonic clock that keeps counting while the device sleeps (or, where the platform's does not, treating each wake as a restart), and across restarts the wall-clock interval since its last checkpoint. If it cannot establish that interval, for example because the clock moved backward, it treats its coverage age as exceeding any limit until the frontier next advances.
- **Historical gaps.** Every gap in the account's history is kept permanently: a commit the witness could not reconstruct, a stream gap, or an interval the witness lacks, whether before its history starts or older than its retention. Each one raises its own gap warning naming the commit or interval. The user can acknowledge a gap warning; that acknowledges only that gap, and never suppresses warnings about later gaps or the liveness warning.

A witness that answers quickly with stale or partial history does not advance the frontier: coverage, not a successful response, is what counts.

### Watching your own account

Each installation MUST follow its own account's history through its witness, persisting its monitoring frontier and the cursor up to which it has processed that history. It applies the baseline [own-account notices](#direct-sync) and alarms to every device record and approval that appears in **any** reconstructed commit, not only the latest, so a device the PDS publishes and then deletes still alarms. It does the same for deletions of its account's device records, which is how it learns of a reset its PDS hides from it. An approval that appears after its signer's device record was deleted, which only a removed device's key could produce ([revoked signers](device-enrollment.md#approval-validation)), raises an alarm, not just a notice.

Where the history starts:

- **Fresh enrollment.** Replay the account's history from its first event on the reference relay, and present every binding that appeared in it, including deleted ones, for the user to recognize, as [starting over](device-enrollment.md#total-device-loss) does for current bindings. A brand-new account should have none; one that does was impersonated before its first device existed, for example around a [pending invite](core.md#invites). Any part of that history the witness lacks raises a gap warning, so an impersonation older than the witness's history is not silently lost.
- **Restore or pairing.** Resume from the cursor in the backup or transferred state, not from the moment of restoring, and replay everything since. Any part of the history after that cursor that the witness lacks, because it pruned it or its history starts later, raises a gap warning for that interval.

### Liveness and deadline

An installation MUST show a liveness warning whenever its coverage age for its own account reaches 24 hours, naming what failed: the witness is unreachable, the witness is behind the relay, or a fetch for the account is outstanding. A clock fault the witness reports shows the warning at once, without waiting for coverage age to reach 24 hours. On launch it MAY first spend up to a minute catching up. The deadline runs from the frontier, not from when a check completes, so neither a freshness allowance nor an outstanding fetch can stretch it: a device accepted at any moment is either processed, and alarms, or leaves the coverage age at 24 hours, and warns, within 24 hours.

Installations SHOULD advance the frontier at least hourly while running, so that the warning shows only for real failures. The warning is not an integrity alarm, since an outage looks the same as withholding. It clears when the coverage age falls back under 24 hours, and it MUST NOT be dismissible while it lasts. Historical gap warnings are separate and do not affect it.
